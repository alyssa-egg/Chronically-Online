# The Fandom Files: collectibles and return visits

## Included in this build

Fourteen original artifact cards with locked silhouettes, clear requirements and progress; a short card reveal and original optional chime; collectible PNG sharing without puzzle answers; a featured find and up to three pinned cards on the Sideblog; homepage open tabs and a next-collectible goal; a searchable Lore Book with related recovered pages. Existing blog themes, daily result sharing, era investigations and reset countdowns stay available. Seven-day rewards count distinct play days without requiring a streak; owned badges are not removed for missing a day. Rarity describes effort, not random chance.

The Lore Book is the selected fourth priority from your “Lore Book or Redacted File” recommendation. A separate Redacted File game, public community profiles, drag-and-drop layouts, and every proposed cosmetic theme are future experiments. No licensed art or music from the reference products is bundled; the artifact illustrations are original SVGs.

The website name is now **The Fandom Files**. The old `deep-cut-*` storage keys and `deep_cut_memberships` table remain internal compatibility identifiers so existing progress and purchases are preserved. New questions continue to require owner approval.

3. Additional ideas that could make the game distinctive
These are the ideas I'd explore beyond badges.

01. The Lore Book

High priority
Every time a player solves a puzzle, they add a page to a digital book of internet history. Pages include the reference, the era, related fandoms, and a short explanation of why it mattered.

Over time, the player builds their own personal archive. This also gives your paid archive a tangible purpose: unlock more pages, stories, and challenges.

02. Mutual Check

After finishing a game, players receive a shareable result card with a funny title and their score.

Examples:

I know too much about fictional men.

Former Tumblr kid, current liability.

I remember the lore so you don't have to.

Add a spoiler-free daily result so friends can compare without giving away answers.

03. The Rabbit Hole

One answer unlocks a related mystery. Solving that mystery reveals another reference, forming a chain.

For example: a band lyric → a Tumblr-era meme → a viral interview → an iconic fandom moment.

This is ideal for your premium archive because people can buy entire themed investigations instead of isolated quizzes.

04. Unlockable Blog Themes

Let players earn custom themes through gameplay rather than simply purchasing every cosmetic.

Think early Tumblr, glittery pop girl, dark academia, 2014 One Direction fan account, chaotic stan Twitter, or early YouTube.

Completing an era could unlock a matching theme, stickers, and a profile header. Make the rewards available to free and premium players, with additional sets in the archive.

05. The Redacted File

A special mystery begins with blacked-out text, blurred images, and missing context. Each correct answer restores part of the file.

Players gradually reconstruct the event until the final reveal. This could become your signature format, particularly if it uses verified screenshots, contextualized historical references, and carefully designed visual clues.

5. Give players a reason to return tomorrow
I would build a simple loop:

Play today's games

Two quick daily challenges

Unlock a collectible

Add it to your Sideblog or Lore Book

Discover a new lead

Preview a mystery or explore the archive

Come back tomorrow

A new daily puzzle and a new chance to collect

I'd also use streaks gently. Give people a bonus for consecutive days, but let them keep their collection even if they miss one. You want the site to feel like a fun internet ritual, not another chore.

What I'd actually build first
If I were prioritizing this for your current site, I'd do these four things first:

The Sideblog collection page — the most visually distinctive part of the experience.

Beautiful collectible cards with locked states — makes progress visible and creates goals.

A satisfying badge-unlock animation — makes the reward feel real.

The Lore Book or Redacted File — gives your game a recognizable mechanic beyond standard trivia.

The bigger idea is that people shouldn't just leave thinking, I got eight out of ten questions right. They should leave thinking, I found something, I added it to my collection, and now I want to see what tomorrow's drop is.

That is what can turn this from a quiz website into its own little fandom universe.
