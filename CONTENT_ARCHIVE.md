How I'd turn this into the actual game
Rather than simply putting all the bullet points into a list, give each topic its own type of playable file.

The Connection File
Match creators to their friend groups, collaborators, exes, or former channels.
Example: Who was in the Sister Squad?




The Timeline File
Put events, breakups, scandals, or viral moments in chronological order.
Example: Reconstruct the TanaCon timeline.




The Sound File
Identify a song from a lyric, an opening line, an album cover, or a cultural association.
Example: Identify the SoundCloud-era song from a lyric.




The Quote File
Identify who said an iconic quote, which video it came from, or what meme it became.
Example: Match the quote to the creator or viral video.




The Lost Archive
The hardest, most obscure content: forgotten creators, deleted videos, niche fandoms, obscure lyrics, and references only dedicated fans recognize.
Example: Identify a forgotten creator from an old thumbnail.



The free sample vs. the paid archive
I'd use the same structure for every era so users know what they're getting.
Free sample	Locked archive
A few iconic creator connections	Complete creator relationship maps
Recognizable viral quotes	Obscure quotes and source-video identification
Mainstream songs and artists	Deep-cut lyrics, album eras, and forgotten artists
One short drama timeline	Full event timelines and related creator lore
Popular memes	Forgotten memes and niche community references
One important distinction: don't make every question about whether someone remembers a celebrity. The most satisfying questions should make the player think, “WAIT, I remember that — and I completely forgot how it connected to everything else.”
For the 2017–2019 era especially, I'd prioritize the Vlog Squad, the Sister Squad, SoundCloud rap, Musical.ly, beauty YouTube, Vine's aftermath, and the early TikTok transition. Those categories have enough interconnected lore to support an entire paid archive rather than just a short nostalgia quiz.
